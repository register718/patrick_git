\subsection{Module 3: Ras Activation, Adaptation and Amplification}\label{sec:m3}

\paragraph{Role.}
The earliest polarised response downstream of the G protein is the activation of Ras.
RasG and RasC are \mk{M3-trans}{activated rapidly and transiently by cAMP in aggregation-competent cells}~\cite{kae2004}, and \mk{M3-pi3k}{RasG is the upstream activator of PI3K}~\cite{sasaki2004,takeda2012}.
\mk{M3-adapt}{The activation of RasG shows near-perfect adaptation over a wide range of concentrations}; among the two simple topologies that can produce perfect adaptation, \mk{M3-iffl}{only an incoherent feed-forward loop fitted the measured responses}, in which \mk{M3-prop}{the receptor drives an excitatory and an inhibitory node proportionally}, \mk{M3-gap-glob}{possibly with a RasGAP as the global inhibitor}~\cite{takeda2012}.
An incoherent feed-forward loop of this type has the structure of a local-excitation--global-inhibition (LEGI) gradient sensor, in which \mk{M3-legi}{a local activator and a global inactivator are coordinately controlled by the G protein}~\cite{levchenko2002,ma2004} and can provide \mk{M3-fcd3}{fold-change detection}~\cite{goentoro2009}.
The RasGAP NF1 (DdNF1) is \mk{M3-nf1}{a major regulator of Ras activity and its loss makes Ras activity spatially and temporally unregulated}~\cite{zhang2008}.
\mk{M3-rect}{Ras activation is suppressed when the cAMP concentration falls (``rectification''), which arises naturally in an incoherent feed-forward loop with zero-order ultrasensitivity}~\cite{nakajima2014}.
Finally, the Ras/PI3K/PIP3 network is \mk{M3-excit}{excitable: it fires without external cues}, and \mk{M3-bias}{a stimulus biases threshold and timing}~\cite{fukushima2019,miao2017,devreotes2017,iglesias2012}.
Module~3 therefore combines (i)~a fast G$\beta\gamma$-driven excitor and a slower G$\beta\gamma$-driven global inhibitor (adaptation and spatial comparison), (ii)~a saturable GAP (rectification), (iii)~a PIP3-to-Ras positive feedback (amplification) and (iv)~a delayed negative feedback (reset).
An integral-feedback (antithetic) arm that was tested earlier was removed, because \mk{M3-noint}{integral feedback gives oscillations and stimulus-dependent kinetics that are inconsistent with the measured Ras response}~\cite{takeda2012}.

\paragraph{Reactions.}
\begin{description}\itemsep0pt
\item[3.1/3.2] The excitor GEF$_\mathrm{R}$ is activated by free G$\beta\gamma$ and inactivated with $\tau_E=2$\,s. \mk{M3-gefr}{RasGEFR is the exchange factor of RasG}~\cite{kae2007}, and \mk{M3-kort}{the initial Ras response depends on G$\beta$}~\cite{kortholt2013}. The short relaxation time makes the RasG peak follow the input within seconds.
\item[3.3] The active GEF exchanges GDP for GTP on membrane-bound RasG, which produces RasG-GTP.
\item[3.4e/3.4b/3.5] The inhibitor GAP is activated by G$\beta\gamma$ (3.4e), i.e.\ by the same variable as the excitor, and decays with $\tau_I=30$\,s (3.5). The requirement that both arms read the same input is essential: if they read different upstream variables with different dose dependence, the ratio of excitation to inhibition acquires a dose dependence of its own and the stage inverts instead of adapting (this was found when the inhibitor read the receptor occupancy). A small basal activation (3.4b) represents the stimulus-independent GAP at rest, which is needed because the resting Ras/PIP3 loop otherwise latches on; a basal RasGAP is consistent with \mk{M3-basal-gap}{the enhanced basal Ras activity of GAP-deficient cells}~\cite{zhang2008,xu2021c2gap}.
\item[3.4f] GAP activation by the phosphorylated receptor. It is defined and switched off ($k_{I,p}=0$): it reads a different upstream variable from 3.1 and inverted the response above about 10\,nM.
\item[3.7z] The active GAP hydrolyses RasG-GTP with Michaelis--Menten kinetics. Because $K_\mathrm{NF1}$ is far below the Ras pool, the enzyme operates in the zero-order regime, where a graded input is turned into a switch-like output and \mk{M3-rect}{the response to a falling stimulus is suppressed}~\cite{nakajima2014}. \mk{M3-nf1kin}{The Michaelis constant of the NF1 GAP domain is $0.3\,\mu$M and its $k_\mathrm{cat}$ is $1.4$\,s$^{-1}$ (mammalian catalytic domain)}~\cite{wiesmuller1992,coyle2016}. The plain mass-action form of this step is available and is not used.
\item[3.3b] PIP3 promotes the exchange on RasG. Together with 4.1 (RasG-GTP recruits PI3K) and 4.3 (PI3K makes PIP3) this closes the \mk{M3-pfb}{Ras$\to$PI3K$\to$PIP3$\to$Ras positive-feedback loop, which is the amplifier of the excitable network}~\cite{sasaki2004,fukushima2019}. The LEGI comparison of 3.1--3.7 provides the spatial signal but no amplification; \mk{M3-kat}{the amplification sits between the G protein and Ras}~\cite{kataria2013}.
\item[3.3c/d/e] A delayed negative feedback that terminates the patch and prevents the positive feedback from latching. \mk{M3-ch-adapt}{In cells lacking PKB and PKBR1, RasC activation is increased and fails to adapt by 40\,s}~\cite{charest2010}, and \mk{M3-miao-ext}{the negative regulation by the PKBs extends to other Ras proteins and to PI3K, which makes them candidates for the delayed negative feedback of the excitable network}~\cite{miao2017}. The brake substrate B is a stand-in for the Sca1-like PKB substrate: it is activated by the membrane kinase PKBR1$^*$ of Module~8 (3.3c), decays with $\tau=10$\,s (3.3d) and accelerates the removal of RasG-GTP (3.3e). \mk{M3-sca1}{PKB phosphorylates the scaffold Sca1 and thereby controls RasC}~\cite{charest2010}; \mk{M3-miao-mech}{the mechanism for the RasG reporter is not identified}~\cite{miao2017}, so applying B to RasG is a lumped assumption.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Constants.}
The rate constants of the LEGI arms (3.1--3.5) are constrained by dynamical targets rather than by single measurements: an excitor time constant of 2\,s, an inhibitor time constant of 30\,s and near-perfect adaptation of the plateau to a sustained step.
The pool sizes were increased to reduce shot noise, and the rate constants were rescaled so that only the products $k_{E,\mathrm{on}}[\mathrm{GEF_R}]_\mathrm{tot}$, $k_{R,\mathrm{on}}[\mathrm{GEF_R}]_\mathrm{tot}$ and $k_{I,\mathrm{on}}[\mathrm{GAP}]_\mathrm{tot}$ enter the dynamics; the pool enlargement in Module~2 was paired with rescaling of $k_{E,\mathrm{on}}$ and $k_{I,\mathrm{on}}$ so that the activated fractions and time constants do not change.
Unpaired, the enlargement makes the stage worse, since the activated fraction of the excitor changes with the G$\beta\gamma$ level.
$K_\mathrm{NF1}$ is taken from the mammalian catalytic domain because no \emph{Dictyostelium} kinetics exist; the $k_\mathrm{cat}$ of the model is derived from the distance to the zero-order switch and is 11 times the mammalian value, so the active-GAP pool stands for more enzyme than is measured.
The feedback gain $k_\mathrm{PIP3}$ cannot be raised as a means to improve gradient contrast: because the feedback acts on the level, it lifts the resting baseline as much as the driven response, and a contrast is a ratio; above a critical gain the loop is bistable and stops resetting.
The brake constants are design targets, not measurements: the activation is derived from a half-activation level of 20 PKBR1$^*$ molecules per voxel and the removal rate from a target strength (0.6 of the LEGI GAP arm), so that the brake is off at rest and engages within a patch.
The brake and the GAP have a cytosolic diffusion constant of $20\,\mu\mathrm{m^2/s}$, which is appropriate for a cytosolic GAP but not for the membrane-associated brake B$^*$ of the code; it makes the brake global rather than local, so that it subtracts the same amount everywhere and leaves the spatial contrast standing, and this is a caveat of the present parameterisation.
